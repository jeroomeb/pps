# Lead generation, recruitment, and outreach

What this is, and how it works. No technical setup is described here.

Amenity Op’s uses this to find two kinds of people, then contact them on a set schedule:

1. **Customers.** Property companies that would subscribe to the software.
2. **Specialists.** People who can run overnight inspections and checklists.

Those two searches stay separate. A building is never treated as a job applicant, and a job applicant is never put on the sales email list.

Sales email does not go out from `amenityops.app`. It goes from separate domains bought only for outreach, after those domains have been warmed up for two to three weeks.

---

## Who a customer is

The first customers are property managers who already have staff and want those people to follow a digital overnight checklist. The software does not replace their team. It gives that team a standard way to inspect the building and a morning report.

The buildings worth contacting are:

- Luxury condominiums and high-rises
- 55+ active-adult and independent-living communities
- Commercial buildings with several tenants
- Regional firms that manage many of those buildings

A building ranks higher when it shows a real problem, not just a name and address.

**Strongest signs, in order**

1. They are hiring for night work: night manager, overnight operations, or front-desk closer.
2. The same company is posting those jobs at several buildings.
3. A decision-maker can be identified at that building.
4. Google reviews mention an empty desk, a broken gate, or a mess in the morning.
5. Employee reviews mention short staffing, overnight burnout, or paperwork done by hand.
6. The city recently fined the building for trash, lighting, or a blocked exit.
7. The owner or management company changed in the last six months.

A plain directory of buildings is only the starting list. It is used to learn the address, size, and owner. It is not treated as a hot lead by itself.

**A building is kept when it matches at least one of these**

- Three or more tenants, or larger than 50,000 square feet
- Luxury amenities such as a concierge, fitness center, clubhouse, underground parking, or controlled access
- Listed as 55+ or independent living

Anything that is not a multi-tenant or premium property is dropped and is not emailed.

## Where customer leads come from

Sources are used in this order. Higher on the list means the building is more likely to need the product now.

**Active pain.** Indeed, ZipRecruiter, LinkedIn, Google Maps reviews, and Glassdoor. These show hiring, complaints, and burnout.

**Risk and compliance.** City building departments, then contact databases that turn a building into a real person’s email. A recent fine is a reason to talk about a digital record of inspections.

**Luxury and 55+ lists.** Senior-housing sites, apartment listing sites, and institutional property reports. These narrow the search to the building types Amenity Op’s is built for.

**Large managers.** Portfolio sites for firms such as Greystar, Lincoln, CBRE, JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, and Colliers. One yes here can mean many buildings.

**Background lists only.** CoStar, LoopNet, Crexi, county property records, state business registries, and similar databases. Use these to learn who owns the building and how big it is. Do not start the email sequence from these lists alone.

## Who gets the sales email

Contact the person who feels the morning problem first.

**At the building**

- Property manager or senior community manager. They deal with overnight staff, paperwork, and the morning handoff.
- General manager or resident-experience director. They hear complaints about gates, doors, and common areas.
- Assistant property manager. They collect the night logs.

**Across several buildings**

- Regional operations director
- VP of property management
- Director of asset management

**Risk and compliance, mostly at large firms**

- Facilities director
- Head of security or compliance
- Director of property technology
- VP of human resources, when night-shift turnover is the problem

Each company gets one primary contact. The sequence is about that person and that property, not a generic blast.

## How the sales emails work

Each company receives four emails, then the sequence stops. If they reply, book a demo, or ask to be left alone, the remaining emails are cancelled immediately.

| Email | When | What it says |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Day 1, mid-morning, Tuesday to Thursday | Introduces Amenity Op’s. Offers a short self-paced demo video. Asks permission to send the link. |
| 2 | Day 4 | Follows up on the morning-surprise problem. Asks them to reply “demo” for the video link. |
| 3 | Day 8 | Asks whether night staff are working from memory and paper. Explains that the current staff keep their jobs and gain a checklist. Offers the video again. |
| 4 | Day 14 | A polite close. Says outreach will stop. Leaves the demo link in case they want it later. |

The message in every email is the same idea: the property keeps its own staff, the staff follow a digital overnight checklist, and the manager gets a morning report.

Sending volume, once the extra domains are ready:

- About 125 new companies added each working day
- About 2,500 companies contacted in a month
- Four emails per company, so most of the daily mail is follow-up, not first contact
- Each outreach inbox sends no more than 50 emails a day
- About 8 to 10 inboxes, on 4 or 5 extra domains
- Target result: about 5 new customers a month

## Who a specialist candidate is

This search fills the field team. It does not sell the software.

A useful candidate has some mix of:

- Night, evening, or early-morning work done without a supervisor nearby
- Facilities, security, loss prevention, or inspection experience
- Shift handoffs and written continuity reports
- Checklist or compliance work, such as fire safety or building conditions
- Comfort with a phone-based checklist and a digital report
- A history of staying in unsupervised jobs, and of being exact about the work

**Where candidates are found**

- LinkedIn, for property, security, and night-operations profiles
- Indeed and ZipRecruiter, for people who have done those shifts
- Security and operations job boards
- Property-software communities, for people who already use building systems

A resume is scored against night operations, asset protection, and checklist experience. Only people who clear that bar are emailed.

They receive two emails, then the sequence stops. There is no sales call in this pipeline. A reply or a request to stop cancels the second email.

The two recruitment messages are not written yet. They should invite the person into an operations role that uses the mobile checklist, and they should not describe the job as a security-guard post.

## What a normal day looks like

1. New buildings and new candidates are collected from the sources above.
2. Buildings that are not luxury, 55+, or multi-tenant are discarded.
3. A building with a hiring post, a complaint, a recent fine, or a new manager is marked as ready to contact.
4. A name and email are attached. Property manager first.
5. Email 1 goes out on the next Tuesday, Wednesday, or Thursday morning.
6. Emails 2, 3, and 4 follow on days 4, 8, and 14 unless the person responds.
7. Separately, qualified specialist candidates receive their own two-email sequence.
8. Replies, demo requests, and “do not contact” requests stop that sequence the same day.

The inspection app and this outreach engine stay separate. Outreach does not send the inspection report email, and the inspection report email does not go out through the sales inboxes.
